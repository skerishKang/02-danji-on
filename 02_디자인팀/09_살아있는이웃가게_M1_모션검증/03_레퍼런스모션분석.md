## Reference A — Scene opening and camera continuity

### Valid segment

Approximately 1.2–27.4s.

### Scene structure

The sequence repeatedly moves through three spatial states:

1. A bright, open foreground made of foliage and a distant horizon.
2. A darker cave-like aperture that closes the visual field around the center.
3. An open cloud plane where white UI cards appear in the middle distance.

The page header remains visually stable while the spatial scene changes underneath it. The interface therefore feels attached to a continuous place rather than replaced by unrelated slides.

### Camera and depth

- Foreground: dark leaves, rock or other frame elements enter from the edges.
- Middle ground: the aperture and main brand copy move with a moderate crop change.
- Background: horizon and clouds move more slowly, creating perceived depth.
- The camera sensation is primarily generated by scale, masking and crop, not by aggressive rotation.
- Transitions are centered around a visible opening, so the eye always has a destination.

### Text and UI

- Large text is anchored to a stable side of the frame.
- Secondary text and header controls remain restrained.
- In the cloud state, functional cards appear after the background opens; the UI does not lead the scene.
- The functional cards are clearly readable because they occupy a calmer middle plane.

### Transition timing observed

The recording repeats the portal-to-card rhythm several times. Major transition clusters appear around 1.2s, 9s, 15s and 23s. Individual crop and aperture changes typically complete in roughly 700–1,100ms, while slow camera drift continues after the transition.

### Danjion translation

Use:

- a persistent product header,
- one stable visual target,
- foreground/middle/background layers,
- masks that open from the working action,
- short functional panels appearing after the scene stabilizes,
- continuity between scene color, photo crop, service name and action.

Do not use:

- forest, cave or portal imagery,
- heavenly landscapes,
- finance cards,
- the original copy or brand,
- continuous automatic scene playback,
- a full-screen film that hides search and service selection.

## Reference B — Selected-space detail layer

### Valid segment

Approximately 1.2–41.8s.

### Base scene

A dark hero scene remains visible behind every selected-state panel. The principal object and oversized title create a recognisable home state. Selection does not navigate to a new page; it temporarily changes focus inside the existing scene.

### Focus transition

When a user selects a space:

1. The base scene darkens.
2. The background loses contrast and receives a light blur.
3. A compact information panel opens above the scene.
4. The panel contains one strong image, short structured facts and a clear close or next action.
5. Closing restores the original focus without changing the page position.

### Timing observed

The recording demonstrates repeated open/close cycles. The background focus shift is fast, approximately 250–450ms. The selected panel follows in roughly 350–600ms. The panel stays visually still after opening. The user can identify the selected state immediately because focus, contrast and panel position change together.

### Danjion translation

Use:

- selected-shop detail as an overlay layer inside the living scene,
- background dimming and limited blur,
- a short transition before updated facts appear,
- stable close, save and detail actions,
- focus return to the triggering shop button.

Do not use:

- a black luxury-club visual language,
- Japanese typography,
- membership gating,
- full-site dark mode,
- hidden navigation,
- oversized decorative words that compete with service information.

## Padiem scroll technique reference

The Padiem reference separates three responsibilities:

1. A fixed visual layer behind the page.
2. Long document flow that supplies normal browser scrolling.
3. A smoothed scroll-progress value that updates visual progress and scene state.

Useful techniques:

- fixed or sticky visual stage,
- document-based scrolling rather than scroll hijacking,
- a thin progress indicator,
- reveal classes for short typography entrances,
- `prefers-reduced-motion` fallback,
- independent product controls above the cinematic layer.

The Motion Gate adapts this technique only in Scene 02 on desktop. Mobile uses explicit scene tabs and short CSS transitions instead of scroll scrubbing.
